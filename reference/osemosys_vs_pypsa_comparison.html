01 · Verdict

The two models found the same answer

Installed capacity matches to six decimal places in every year. Total generation matches to eight significant figures. Both models build exactly the same 103.474 GW of new coal and nothing else — with every impact, emission and carbon price stripped out of this benchmark, coal is the only technology either optimiser chooses to invest in. Every other technology exists purely as fixed residual capacity.

Where the solutions do drift apart is dispatch routing, and the amount is small and structural: the LP optimum is a large degenerate face, so many equally-optimal ways of splitting flow exist and the two solvers land on different vertices of the same face.

02 · Cost · why the objectives differ

The 1.68% gap is two discounting conventions

Decomposing both objectives into matching components shows the gap is not concentrated anywhere — every component is uniformly a little higher in PyPSA, for two known reasons.

Convention 1 — annuity vs annuity-due, on capex

PyPSA's annuity(r,N) = r/(1−(1+r)⁻ᴺ); OSeMOSYS's capital recovery factor is the same expression divided by (1+r). So PyPSA should charge exactly (1+r_idv)× OSeMOSYS's capital cost. Coal is the only technology built, and it carries r_idv = 3.10% — predicting a ratio of 1.0310 against an observed 1.0302, an error of 0.08%.

That near-exact match also confirms a second thing: PyPSA truncating the annuity at the last modelled period is equivalent to OSeMOSYS's sinking-fund salvage credit. OSeMOSYS books $147,437m of gross capex and credits $79,667m of salvage; PyPSA never books either, and still lands within 0.08% of the same net figure.

Convention 2 — mid-year vs start-of-year operating costs

OSeMOSYS discounts opex at (1+r)^−(y−y₀+0.5), PyPSA at (1+r)^−(y−y₀). At the social discount rate r = 3% that predicts a ratio of 1.03^0.5 = 1.01489. Observed: 1.0152 on variable O&M and 1.0141 on fixed O&M. The small residual on fixed O&M is the third, much smaller convention — PyPSA's fom_cost is a scalar frozen at the build year, while OSeMOSYS re-applies FixedCost[y] annually.

03 · Cost · the reporting trap

The $364bn that vanishes from the LP

There is a second, much larger discrepancy that has nothing to do with the optimum — it is purely about which number each framework calls "the objective".

tz-osemosys builds a TotalDiscountedCost expression that includes fixed O&M on residual capacity. That term is a constant — residual capacity is exogenous, so no decision changes it. linopy cannot carry a constant in an objective, so objective.py zeroes it out before handing the LP to the solver:

tz/osemosys/model/objective.py

if (objective.const != 0).any():
  objective_constant = objective.const.item()
  objective.const.values = 0  # ← stripped before solving

And model.py then recomputes Model.objective from the full expression after solving — with the constant — leaving an open TODO: find out why and add constant back on. The two numbers are not the same quantity.

The consequence: the HiGHS log reports 597,457 $m, but model.solution.nc's own TotalDiscountedCost sums to 961,739 $m — 61% higher. The difference is 364,282 $m of discounted fixed O&M on residual capacity, which I isolated by splitting each technology's fixed O&M by its residual-vs-new capacity share. It reconciles to the dollar.

PyPSA is missing the same cost category, for a different and cleaner reason: its objective only ever assembles fixed costs from extendable components, and all 2,007 residual generators in the network carry fom_cost = 0 explicitly.

How to compare these two models

Read the objective off the solver log on both sides — that pairing (597,457 vs 607,473) is like-for-like, and both are structurally missing residual fixed O&M. Never compare tz-osemosys's Model.objective or TotalDiscountedCost against PyPSA's n.objective: that mixes a with-constant number against a without-constant one and makes OSeMOSYS look 58% more expensive, not less.

Cost by year needs splitting first

Total cost per year is not directly comparable between the two frameworks, and plotting it as-is would invent a disagreement that doesn't exist. OSeMOSYS books capital investment in full in the build year and credits salvage at the end of the horizon; PyPSA charges an annuity spread across every year the asset is active. In 2023 that is $26,163m of OSeMOSYS capex against $1,395m of PyPSA annuity — a 37% apparent gap that is entirely accounting timing.

Separating the two components makes both readable. Operating cost — the genuinely comparable part — tracks at a near-constant ratio of 1.0148 to 1.0163 across all 28 years, which is the mid-year discounting convention and nothing else.

05 · Capacity

Capacity is effectively identical

Summed across all technologies, regions and years, installed capacity differs by 3.60 GW out of 7,031 GW — 0.05%, and the largest single cell disagreement anywhere is 0.29 GW. The entire difference sits in coal; every other technology agrees to floating-point noise (largest deviation 1.2×10⁻¹⁴ GW).

New capacity shows a larger relative spread (3.41%) despite identical totals, because the two solvers place the same 103.474 GW of coal in slightly different build years — a vintage-timing choice the LP is close to indifferent about.

06 · Generation

Same totals, slightly different dispatch

Total generation over the horizon agrees to eight significant figures (27,440.92 TWh both sides), and every year matches to within 8×10⁻⁶ TWh. The 0.60% Σ|Δ| is pure redistribution: PyPSA runs 6.05 TWh less coal and makes it up with 4.06 TWh more gas and 1.97 TWh more nuclear.

Geographically the swap is concentrated: Hokkaido generates 22.9 TWh less under PyPSA and Tokyo 22.2 TWh more, with a smaller Kyushu/Shikoku exchange. Every variable-renewable technology — solar, wind, hydro, geothermal — agrees to floating-point noise, because their output is pinned by p_max_pu rather than chosen.

07 · Interconnectors

Trade routing is where they genuinely differ

Gross interconnector flow agrees closely in total — 1,563 TWh against 1,565 TWh — but Σ|Δ| reaches 9.35%, by far the largest disagreement of any metric. This is the degeneracy that the solve-time analysis already flagged: with ~14.5 identical vintages per technology, enormous numbers of flow patterns are exactly equally optimal.

The single largest divergence is a routing substitution around Tohoku: PyPSA sends 24.4 TWh less on Tohoku→Tokyo and 22.7 TWh more on Tohoku→Hokkaido. A second pair swaps Shikoku→Chugoku against Kyushu→Chugoku by about 12 TWh each. In both cases the system serves identical load at identical cost by a different path.

08 · Method

How these numbers were derived

  • Sources. ce_pypsa_benchmarking/model.solution.nc (tz-osemosys solution) and ce_solved_network.nc (PyPSA 1.2.4, multi_investment_periods=True).
  • Units. OSeMOSYS is GW / TWh / $m; PyPSA is MW / MWh / $. PyPSA figures are divided by 10³ for capacity, 10⁶ for energy and 10⁶ for cost.
  • Capacity. OSeMOSYS GrossCapacity against PyPSA p_nom_opt masked by get_active_assets per investment period, so retired vintages are excluded.
  • Generation. OSeMOSYS TotalTechnologyAnnualActivity against PyPSA generators_t.p weighted by snapshot_weightings.objective and grouped by period.
  • Trade. OSeMOSYS ExportAnnual against PyPSA links_t.p0 (flow into bus0, before losses) on the matching directed route.
  • Cost. PyPSA's capital_cost is stored as 0 in this network — the value is derived at optimize time — so capex was rebuilt as annuity(discount_rate, lifetime) × overnight_cost and charged over active periods weighted by investment_period_weightings. The reconstruction reproduces n.objective to a ratio of 1.000000, which is what makes the component-level comparison trustworthy.
  • The constant. Residual fixed O&M was isolated by scaling DiscountedFixedOperatingCost by each cell's ResidualCapacity / GrossCapacity share. It sums to 364,282.4 $m against a 597,457-to-961,739 gap of 364,282.4 $m.
